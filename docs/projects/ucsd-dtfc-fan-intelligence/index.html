Objective & data

Organization background
Dallas Trinity FC is a professional women’s soccer club that launched as an expansion team in 2024, so it had only limited historical fan data to work with.

Objective & data provided
Create a Brand Affinity Score (BAS) from ticket and merchandise sales over the club’s first two seasons.

Key business questions

At its core, the club wanted to answer one question: what is a fan?

Connection
Which fans are building the strongest connection to DTFC?

Engagement
How do we measure and understand fan engagement?

Loyalty
How do we keep fans coming back over time?

How the models work together

The BAS was the core deliverable. I paired it with predictive models so that each business question has a score behind it:

Brand Affinity Score (Fan+) · Connection
Who is this fan today? Scores each fan’s connection to the club from ticket and merchandise behavior, used to prioritize loyalty-program outreach.

Segment change · Loyalty
Where is the relationship heading? Probability that a fan upgrades or downgrades over the next four home games.

Ticket propensity · Engagement
How likely are they to buy right now? Probability of buying at least one ticket for the next four home games.

Growth & retention · Engagement & loyalty
How many games will they attend? Expected attendance compared with the fan’s own recent baseline.

Pipeline

Fan data core: tickets & merchandise (2 seasons); enrichment: email, YouTube, TV, schedule

Feature engineering time-aligned to a snapshot date to prevent leakage

Rolling time-based CV train on past windows, test on the next

Models XGBoost vs. logistic and heuristic baselines

Explainability SHAP and permutation importance

CRM master file scores → action segments

Validating honestly. Fan behavior is seasonal and the data spans two seasons, so a random split would leak future information into training. I validated with rolling-origin folds instead: each model trains on earlier home-game windows and is tested on the next window. For the segment-change model, XGBoost beat both a rules-based heuristic and regularized logistic regression on AUC in every fold.

Results

Headline validation, taken from the final client deliverable:

Model Question Validation
Segment change Upgrade or downgrade in the next 4 games? Upgrade AUC 0.91 (43% upgrade rate in the top 10%); downgrade AUC 0.95 (~20× lift in the top 5%)
Ticket propensity Buy ≥ 1 ticket in the next 4 games? AUC 0.833; 4.3× lift in the top 15%
Growth & retention Attend more or fewer games than baseline? AUC 0.715; 5.5× lift for fans attending 2+ games

From scores to action

A model only creates value if the marketing team can use it. I translated the probability scores into plain-language segments, such as Upgrade Candidate, Retention Watchlist, and Stable / Low-Touch, and wrote them into a single CRM master file. The handoff package included:

  • an Analytical Guide that explains each model, its intended use, and its limits in non-technical language
  • a navigation guide to the deliverables and model-specific methodology notes
  • a reproducible refresh script, so the club can re-score fans each season without the project team
  • an executive presentation to the club’s senior marketing and data-strategy leadership

Lessons

  • Handoff design is part of the model. Documentation, segment labels, and a refresh path mattered as much as AUC.
  • Explainability builds trust. SHAP and permutation importance let non-technical stakeholders see why a fan was flagged.
  • Time alignment is the hardest part. Joining six sources with different grains and horizons required explicit snapshot logic to avoid leakage.
