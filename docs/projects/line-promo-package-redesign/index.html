Business context

To drive revenue during a period of low market demand, the product team launched a limited-time promotional ad package with bundles tailored to different client groups. On paper the design was careful and incorporated sales feedback, but after launch it significantly underperformed, and senior management started to worry.

The package belonged to another team. My routine KPI tracking across sales channels showed me the performance gap, and I volunteered to investigate.

My role

  • Identified the problem and took on the analysis without a formal mandate
  • Analyzed sales results across hundreds of clients and several sales channels (Excel, Tableau)
  • Diagnosed the structural causes and proposed a revised offer and launch timing
  • Partnered with the product manager to refine the plan and win buy-in, presenting it as iteration rather than critique

Analytical approach

The team’s first hypothesis was simply “demand is low.” I wanted to know whether the design of the offer was part of the problem.

Low adoption symptom

Segment analysis who is (and isn’t) buying?

Offer analysis does the offer fit each segment?

Timing analysis does launch timing match buying cycles?

Visual explanation

Three lenses on one problem. No single view explained the underperformance. The answer came from looking at customer segments, offer structure, and timing together:

Customer segment
Client groups differ in buying power and buying behavior. Who is the offer actually reaching?

Offer structure
The incentive has to be relevant to each group. Is the value concentrated where it drives the most incremental purchases?

Timing
Buying cycles differ by group. Does the launch window match when each group plans and spends?

Diagnosis
The analysis found two structural design flaws: one in how the offer was distributed across client groups, and one in how launch timing lined up with their buying cycles.

The redesign tailored the offer and its timing to each client group, based on that group’s buying power and past seasonal behavior.

Business impact

  • The redesigned package delivered about 20× the sales of the original launch
  • The analysis was later expanded into a standing framework for clients’ annual deals, used to assess each client’s true spending power rather than relying on past order size alone
  • It showed how cross-channel KPI tracking can reveal structural issues that are hard to see from inside a single team
  • The product manager who championed the revision later moved into a product-lead role and asked their team to keep working with me on key initiatives
