Executive summary

For my MSBA consulting capstone, I built a predictive analytics workflow for a professional sports organization and turned its output into a framework that marketing teams could use to prioritize outreach.

Business challenge

A professional sports organization sought a more systematic approach to understanding fan engagement and prioritizing customer outreach.

My role

  • Prepared the data and engineered time-aligned features
  • Built and evaluated XGBoost models with time-aware validation
  • Used SHAP to interpret the models and explain the results
  • Translated model scores into CRM decisions a marketing team could act on

Analytical approach

I built a predictive analytics workflow using historical engagement and transaction signals, evaluated models with time-aware validation, and used SHAP to translate predictions into interpretable business insights.

Validating honestly. Fan behavior changes over time, so a random split would leak future information into training. I validated with rolling-origin folds instead: each model trains on earlier windows and is tested on the next one, and I compared XGBoost against simpler baselines.

Explaining the model. SHAP breaks each prediction into the contribution of each input. In general terms, that lets a non-technical stakeholder see why a customer received a score, as well as the score itself.

Outcome

Delivered a framework for audience prioritization and more targeted CRM engagement.

Lessons learned

  • Handoff design is part of the model. Documentation and clear labels mattered as much as model performance.
  • Explainability builds trust. SHAP helped non-technical stakeholders see why a customer was flagged.
  • Time alignment is the hardest part. Combining sources with different grains and horizons required explicit snapshot logic to avoid leakage.
